## Why I Am Using LabEx.io

I have been using **LabEx.io** as one of my Linux learning tools, especially for hands-on practice.

Reading documentation is useful.
Watching tutorials is useful.
But at some point Linux looks at you politely and says:

> "Nice theory. Now open the terminal."

That is where LabEx fits nicely for me.
It gives me a practical environment where I can work through Linux exercises, build command-line confidence, and track my progress without needing to rebuild my own lab every time I want to test something.

My LabEx profile is here:
[Check out my LabEx profile, including hands-on learning progress, badges, and skills.](https://labex.io/u/erik-sleziona-01097906)

## Hands-On Practice Matters

The main reason I like this kind of platform is simple:
Linux is not something you learn only by reading about it.

You need to type commands.
You need to make small mistakes.
You need to read output, adjust, try again, and occasionally wonder why one missing slash can change your whole evening.

That is part of the learning process.
LabEx makes that process easier to repeat because the exercises are structured, the environment is ready, and the feedback is immediate enough to keep momentum.

For someone learning system administration, that repetition matters a lot.
Commands such as `ls`, `cd`, `cat`, `grep`, `find`, permissions, file paths, package management, and basic troubleshooting all become less intimidating when they stop being abstract ideas and start becoming normal habits.

## Progress, Badges, And Skills

One thing I also like is that LabEx gives a visible record of progress.

The profile shows learning activity, badges, and skills, which is useful because it turns practice into something measurable.
It is not just "I watched a video once and felt inspired for six minutes."
It is more like:

- I completed practical exercises
- I built repeatable Linux habits
- I can see what areas I have worked on
- I have a reason to keep going

That little progress trail helps.
Learning Linux can feel wide, especially when every new topic opens three more topics hiding behind it.
Having a structured path keeps the chaos slightly more polite.

## How This Connects To My Homelab

LabEx is not replacing my homelab.
It is helping me prepare for it.

My homelab is still very much part of the plan, but the next stage is waiting on new hardware.
So for now, while I wait for the new equipment to arrive, I am using LabEx to keep practicing Linux and sharpening the skills that will be useful once the hardware is ready.

In other words:
the homelab story is not cancelled.
It is just in the very professional sysadmin phase called:

> "Waiting for delivery and pretending not to check tracking every 20 minutes."

Once the new gear arrives, I will continue the homelab work and document the next steps here.
That should include more Linux practice, server configuration, networking, automation, and probably at least one moment where I say, "This should only take five minutes" before learning something important the hard way.

## Final Thought

For now, LabEx.io is a useful part of my learning routine.
It gives me a place to practice Linux in a structured way, follow my progress, collect badges, and keep building the command-line confidence I need for real homelab projects.

The next homelab chapter is coming.
I am just waiting for the hardware to arrive first.

Until then, the terminal stays open.
